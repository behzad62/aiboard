## Design decisions

- Bridge IDs are deterministic: the scheduler task id equals the ready-plan contract id. `planning.plan_ready` materializes missing contract tasks through the scheduler reducer; replay rebuilds the same task/binding set.
- True membership checks the CURRENT ready revision. A dropped contract remains visibly non-admissible and reaches `droppedReadyContractTasks` / stale-task Architect wake; post-ready `plan_tasks` tasks without a contract are unmapped and refused.
- Kernel repair packets use `repair:<encoded-parent-contract>::<task-id>`, remain admissible under the ready identity, and reserve through their parent contract.
- Capacity is `MAX_WORKERS = 4`, further bounded by configured maximum and optional actual resource/provider capacity. Legacy capacity remains the configured maximum.
- Claim protection is `procedural-single-controller`: each scheduler append is SQLite `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`; the sole scheduler serializes reservation and task/worktree identity before dispatch. The planning reducer independently rejects competing overlapping claims.
- Paths normalize separators, case-fold for comparison, collapse `.`/`..`, and use `realpathSync` alias resolution when the target exists. Shared lockfiles, migrations/config/contracts, and declared semantic resources serialize.
- Restart reconciliation never frees an unconfirmed live claim when a caller ends. Reassignment requires durable `stopped_fenced` state with writer-stop evidence; ambiguous writers block.
- WorkspaceManager records local `aiboard.taskOwner` and refuses a second task owner for one worktree/session.
- Undeclared files route to replan/claim revision; they never silently widen write ownership or grant filesystem permission.
